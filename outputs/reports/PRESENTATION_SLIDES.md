### **WHAT:**

# SLIDE 8: TEMPERATURE EVIDENCE

## 📊 THE PHYSICS OF COLLAPSE

### **3 KEY FACTS:**

1. **Hottest sites are all urban:**
   - W2096 (Neponset, Boston): 27.5°C average (81.5°F)
   - W3012 (SuAsCo, Worcester): 27.0°C (80.6°F)
   - W0776 (Buzzards Bay): 26.9°C (80.5°F)
   - Pattern: Pavement-to-river ratio of 70-80% in these areas

2. **Temperature-oxygen physics:**
   - 15°C water holds 10.0 mg/L oxygen (healthy)
   - 25°C water holds 7.0 mg/L oxygen (30% LESS capacity)
   - Critical threshold: 5 mg/L (survival limit)
   - Urban rivers hit 27°C = physically impossible to sustain aquatic life

3. **Summer concentration:**
   - June-August: 92% of all critical oxygen events
   - Peak danger: July-August heat waves (7+ consecutive days >30°C air temp)
   - Coincides with: Swimming season, family recreation, outdoor activities

# SLIDE 23: ROI EVIDENCE

## 📊 THE BUSINESS CASE FOR PREVENTION

### **3 KEY FACTS:**

1. **Emergency response costs per major fish kill event:**
   - Equipment deployment + staff overtime: $100-300K
   - Dead fish cleanup + disposal: $20-50K
   - Ecosystem assessment + fish restocking: $50-100K
   - Lost recreation revenue (beach/fishing closures): $500K-2M
   - **TOTAL per event: $1-2.5M**
   - **Frequency: 2-3 events/year currently, increasing to 6-10/year by 2045 under climate change**

2. **Prevention infrastructure costs:**
   - 50 continuous dissolved oxygen sensors: $250K (one-time)
   - Real-time monitoring platform + alerts: $100K (one-time)
   - Rapid response team (staff + equipment): $150K/year
   - Sensor maintenance + data analysis: $50K/year
   - **TOTAL: $350K upfront + $200K/year operating**
   - **Annual cost: $500K/year** (including amortization)

3. **ROI calculation:**
   - Prevention cost: $500K/year
   - Emergency costs avoided: 2-3 events × $1.5M avg = $3-4.5M/year
   - Avoided recreation loss: $2-4M/year
   - **Total savings: $5-8.5M/year**
   - **ROI: 10-17x annually | 5-10x over 20-year sensor lifespan**

# SLIDE 24: ROI EQUITY

## ⚖️ WHO BENEFITS FROM PREVENTION?

### **FAIRNESS ANALYSIS:**

**Direct Beneficiaries:**
- **Urban families:** Avoid health hazards (algae blooms, contaminated water)
- **Subsistence fishers:** Avoid toxic fish exposure, maintain cultural food source
- **Low-income recreation users:** Keep free swimming/fishing access open
- **Riverside property owners:** Avoid property value decline (30-50% drop after major fish kills)
- **Municipalities:** Avoid emergency cleanup costs, beach closure economic losses

**Who Pays Currently:**
- General taxpayer funds emergency response → everyone pays equally
- But impacts fall disproportionately on environmental justice communities

**Who Should Pay (Proposed):**
- 40% State general fund (everyone benefits from healthy ecosystems)
- 40% Targeted fees on major polluters (industrial dischargers, large developers)
- 20% Municipal match from communities benefiting

**Data Limitations:**
- ROI calculations based on REPORTED fish kills only (official events)
- Unreported ecosystem damage not monetized:
  - Slow species loss (no dramatic die-off, just gradual decline)
  - Reproductive failure (fish don't die, but can't reproduce in warm water)
  - Recreational value loss hard to quantify in environmental justice communities (no "willingness to pay" surveys)
- True ROI likely HIGHER than 5-10x when including unmeasured benefits

**Equity Question:**
- If polluters pay 40%, do they get preferential treatment in response?
- How to ensure low-income communities get EQUAL protection, not just proportional to what they can pay?
